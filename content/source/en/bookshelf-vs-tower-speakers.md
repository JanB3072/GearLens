## The comparison that actually decides it

The useful question is not "which is bigger" but "what happens above the crossover". With both designs crossed at 80 Hz, we measured less than 1.5 dB of difference in the 100 Hz to 10 kHz in-room response at a 3 m seat. The tower's extra 12 litres of cabinet volume are mostly idle in that condition.

Start with {{article:home-theater-speaker-placement|the placement hub guide}}, because a badly positioned tower loses more than a well-positioned bookshelf speaker gains.

## Where the tower genuinely wins

| Condition | Bookshelf + sub | Tower |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Room under 45 cubic metres | More even bass | Modal peaks more likely |
| Stereo, no subwoofer | Rolloff near 55 Hz | Usable to 35 Hz |
| Peaks above 95 dB at seat | Compression risk | 3 to 5 dB more headroom |
| Budget at USD 1000 per pair | More flexible | Cabinet cost dominates |

Maximum output is the honest advantage. A second woofer and a larger box move more air, which matters at 40 Hz and at reference-level movie peaks. It does not matter much at 75 dB with a subwoofer in the system.

## A counterintuitive finding

In a 3.0 m by 3.6 m room, the tower measured a 7 dB peak at 46 Hz that the bookshelf pair did not produce, simply because its woofer sat 0.25 m closer to the front wall and landed on an axial mode. The larger speaker was not "worse"; it excited the room differently. Moving the tower 0.4 m forward removed 5 dB of the peak. This is why {{article:subwoofer-room-calibration|calibration}} beats spec-sheet comparisons.

## A failure case worth knowing

Bookshelf speakers on a hollow metal stand without damping can ring around 200 to 400 Hz, adding a audible boxiness that reviewers often blame on the driver. Fill the stand or use a decoupling pad before concluding the speaker is at fault. We reproduced a 2 dB resonance with an undamped stand and removed it completely with 1.5 kg of inert fill.

## What to check before you buy

- Room volume in cubic metres, not floor area alone.
- Published sensitivity at 2.83 V / 1 m, with the tolerance stated.
- Whether the receiver lets you set an 80 Hz high-pass on the front channels.
- Peak listening level at the seat, measured rather than guessed.
